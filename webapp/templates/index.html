How this was validated

The accuracy number matters less than whether it means anything. Three problems were found and measured in this dataset.

ProblemWhat was foundStatus
Subject leakage Slices from one person appearing in both training and test data inflates accuracy by 36.9 points — measured directly here. Avoided by splitting per person before any image is created
Scanner confound Every healthy/Alzheimer's subject came from one scanner era and every MCI subject from another, so "diagnosis" and "scanner" were the same variable. Models scored 98–100% at identifying the scanner while at chance on the disease. Fixed by rebuilding the dataset so every class spans both eras; scanner era now gives +0.0% over baseline
Slice misalignment Slices were taken at a fixed fraction of image height, landing on different anatomy per subject — sometimes missing the hippocampus entirely. Re-extracted anchored in millimetres below the top of the skull

The model was then trained on one scanner generation and tested on a completely different one — different machines, years and vendors, no shared subjects. It still ranked Alzheimer's above healthy (AUC 0.68–0.79), which is difficult to explain if it were reading scanner artefacts.

Trained on the ADNI dataset with a strict subject-wise split — no person appears in both training and test data. Uploaded images are processed in memory and are never stored or logged.
Four-stage classification (CN / EMCI / LMCI / AD) is deliberately not offered here: it is not yet reliably better than guessing the most common class, and presenting it would look authoritative without being so.
This tool cannot diagnose anyone. If you have concerns about memory or cognition, speak to a doctor.
